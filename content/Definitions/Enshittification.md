### /ɛnˈʃɪt.ə.fɪ.keɪ.ʃən/ (Phonetic respelling with syllable breaks)

noun
ECONOMICS & TECHNOLOGY; the pattern by which platforms, digital services, and marketplaces progressively degrade in quality as their operators shift focus from serving users to extracting value for business customers and shareholders.

---

## Origins & Etymology

The term was coined in November 2022 by writer, technologist, and activist Cory Doctorow on his blog *Pluralistic*. It quickly gained widespread adoption across tech journalism, economics, and sociology to describe the lifecycle of online platforms. The etymology playfully combines the vulgar noun "shit" with the nominalizing suffix "-fication" (denoting a process of transformation), capturing the inevitable degradation of user experience into something frustrating and unusable.

## Core Characteristics

* **The Platform Capture Cycle:** Platforms progress through a predictable economic trajectory: first, they subsidize and heavily reward **users** to build network effects; second, once users are locked in, they shift value to **business customers** (merchants, advertisers, creators); finally, once both groups are locked in, they allocate all residual value to **shareholders**, leaving users and businesses squeezed.
* **Asymmetric Lock-In:** As a platform degrades—such as through invasive ads, algorithmic suppression, higher fees, or broken features—users find it exceedingly difficult to leave because their social graphs, data, and workflows are trapped inside the ecosystem.
* **Extractive Monopolization:** The phenomenon highlights how modern digital platforms transition from being useful public utilities or open ecosystems into walled gardens designed purely for rent-seeking extraction.
