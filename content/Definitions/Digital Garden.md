### /ˈdɪʒ.ɪ.təl ˈɡɑːr.dən/ (American Heritage standard pronunciation key with syllable spaces)

_noun_  
KNOWLEDGE MANAGEMENT; a collection of evolving, interconnected notes and ideas cultivated openly over time, moving away from rigid chronological publication toward organic growth.

--- 
### Origins & Etymology 
The term draws a deliberate contrast with the traditional "stream" of social media or the static "blog post" archive. While blogs are organized chronologically (like a diary or journal entries where new content buries the old), a digital garden functions like a network of growing spaces. The concept traces its roots back to early web visionaries like Mark Bernstein, who wrote about [Eastgate Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastgate_Systems) and hypertext architectures, and was famously popularized in modern knowledge management by [Vannevar Bush's conceptual Memex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memex). 
### Core Characteristics * 
**Non-Linear Navigation:** Rather than reading from top to bottom like a book, visitors traverse notes via bidirectional links (backlinks), mirroring associative human memory. * **Imperfect & Evolving:** Notes exist in states ranging from "seedling" (rough thoughts) to "budding" (developing arguments) to "evergreen" (polished reference material). Nothing is ever truly "finished." * **Public Thinking:** Unlike private notebooks, digital gardens embrace working in public, inviting serendipitous collaboration and feedback.
